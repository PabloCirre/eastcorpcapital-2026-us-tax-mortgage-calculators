## 1.1 New York State & New York City Statutory Income Tax (Form IT-201)

Residents of New York City face a three-layer income tax stack:
1. **Federal Income Tax (Form 1040):** 10% to 37% progressive brackets plus the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax (Form 8959) and 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax (Form 8960).
2. **New York State Income Tax (NY Tax Law § 601):** 9 progressive brackets from **4.00% to 10.90%**, subject to the **New York Supplemental Tax (Tax Computation Worksheet / Benefit Recapture)**, which strips lower-bracket rate benefits once New York Adjusted Gross Income (NYAGI) exceeds **$107,650** ( recapturing up to the top marginal rate on every dollar from dollar one).
3. **New York City Local Income Tax:** **3.078% to 3.876%** across all five boroughs (Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, The Bronx, and Staten Island).

## 1.2 Wall Street Supplemental Bonus Withholding vs. True Tax Liability

Under **IRS Publication 15 (Circular E)** and **26 CFR § 31.3402(g)-1**, employers withhold federal tax on supplemental wages (cash bonuses, RSUs vesting, signing bonuses, and commissions) using two statutory tiers:
- **22.0% Flat Federal Withholding** on the first **$1,000,000** of supplemental wages paid during the calendar year.
- **37.0% Mandatory Federal Withholding** on all supplemental wages exceeding **$1,000,000**.
- **11.70% New York State** + **4.25% New York City** supplemental withholding rates.

Because an executive earning a $250,000 base salary and a $350,000 bonus ($600,000 total compensation) sits in the **35% or 37% federal marginal bracket**, the 22% statutory withholding creates a **13% to 15% federal under-withholding gap ($45,500+ owed in April)** unless covered by quarterly estimated payments (Form 1040-ES / 110% Safe Harbor rule).
